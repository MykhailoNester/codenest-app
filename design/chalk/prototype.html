The four rules
1 · Borders, never fills
On the board a surface is drawn by its outline. On paper an outline alone reads as a wireframe, so the card carries a tint there and the border steps back.
2 · One ground
No shadow, no gradient, no blur, no glow. Depth is expressed by hairlines and spacing.
3 · Mono carries fact
Numbers, ids, paths, models and time are mono. Prose is sans. You can tell data from label at a glance.
4 · Colour answers one question
Colour means exactly one thing: how much this wants you. Five steps, reused on every surface. Everything else — projects, agents, models, paths, counts — stays mono.

Disabled
With no fills, a dead control and a ghost button are the same picture. Disabled is stated: ink drops to --faint, the border goes to hairline, a filled button surrenders its fill, and nothing answers the pointer.
The ramp
Chalk's own semantic file says its accent is “waiting on you, at most once per screen” and its negative is “never alarming”. So the top of this ramp is a clay, not a red — it is the colour of something unfinished, not of an error dialog.

Where colour is not allowed
The old theme put six hues on every card, so colour stopped carrying information. Why restyling is not enough

The brightness is not only in the token file. Shell paints two 520px radial orbs and a grid overlay in its own JSX; kpi-tile owns a corner glow; the sidebar avatar is a blue-to-violet gradient written inline; the kanban reads its column colours from the sidecar's status_colors map. Swapping tokens.css would leave every one of those in place. That is the argument for rebuilding the primitives rather than repainting them — and the reason the prototype's CSS is 314 lines against the current 12,857.

Decisions that want your answer
How much colour?
Minimal is the recommendation — the five-step ramp and nothing else. None is the control: shape carries the same information, so if Minimal does not beat None on the Work Board the ramp has not earned its place. Full adds identity hues to project and agent marks, and is mostly there to show that a second colour system undoes the first.
Manrope or Inter Tight?
The prototype uses Manrope, which is what the original Chalk uses — adopting it makes the two products visibly one house. Inter Tight is what the app loads today and costs nothing to keep.
Light mode: ship or defer?
The token file carries paper as well as board and the prototype switches cleanly, but nothing in the app reads a theme attribute today — there is no switch, no persistence, no xterm re-theme. That is its own small phase.
Which density ships as default?
Outline cards need more padding to separate than filled ones, so comfortable costs roughly a third of a screen against today's theme. Compact buys it back. Toggle it on the Work Board — if compact is what you actually want, it should be the default rather than a setting.
Sidecar status colours
The board's column colours come from the DB's status_colors map — arbitrary per-row hex the ramp cannot honour. Either the frontend stops reading that field and maps each status to a ramp step, or the seed rows are rewritten to the ramp's five values.
What this prototype is not
Static markup with invented numbers. No routing, no data, no drag, no virtualisation, no xterm. The two stylesheets beside it — tokens.css and chalk.css — are the real deliverable: they are written to be copied into frontend/src/styles/ unchanged.
Not in this prototype
This page exists in the app and keeps its route, its data and its behaviour. It is not drawn here because the five screens that carry the redesign argument are Mission Control, Needs You, the Work Board, Task Detail and Sessions — and a prototype that draws thirty-eight screens is a prototype nobody reads to the end.
Phase 6 in the plan migrates it, one page per commit.
